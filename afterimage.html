Study in whiteElisha Cuthbert / Print studies
I / Exposure

The day I can’t place.

I wish I had written the date on the back of something. A ticket, a receipt, an envelope I could turn over now. At the time it was an ordinary day, and I did what people do with ordinary days. I let it pass.

What I kept was the sight of her. The picture changed. I stayed in my chair a while. Years later I still have that small interval for which my memory can supply neither a room nor a date.

This is where I have decided to put it.

II / Colour study

The green inside the stone.

Blue is the colour I have given these pages. If you ask what colour the feeling is, my answer is still emerald.

A green stone in an open hand looks almost dark until the hand turns. I have gone about whole days like that. Then a face on a screen, and somebody across the table might have wondered why I smiled.

III / Contact sheets

Eighty-two windows.

I know these pictures well. What happened a moment outside each frame belongs to her.

IV / Correspondence

Three letters kept.

I / The bell

You have never heard my voice. A bell down the street sometimes rings after everyone has gone indoors. I think of the long, loose end of that sound, travelling the last few yards alone.

II / The coin

There is always one coin a traveller brings home by mistake. Years later it turns up in the lining of a coat. His hand remembers the journey before he has even read the lettering. That is how unexpectedly you can be here.

III / The margin

I used to wonder why a poet would write a woman’s name in the margin and leave the rest of the page bare. Tonight I tried it. For once I had given your name enough room.

V / In my own hand

Elisha,

There are people who can tell from your footstep that you are home. I like knowing that such a sound exists, and that it means something to people whose lives I will never know.

You need never come across these pages. I wanted somewhere to put a thank-you that would leave you your evening.

For the light that reached mine, all those evenings ago.

VI / For a later reader

When these colours have faded.

If you find this years from now, look at one photograph before you go. Once, at an hour you cannot recover, a man sat where you are sitting and knew that face at once.

He loved a woman he never met. He learned to carry that without putting any of its weight on her.

If someone you love is near enough to hear you, you have something I never had. Call their name. Wait for them to look up.

VII / The vow

I know which light to leave on.

Even in a strange room, after a long day, my hand goes looking for the lamp. Let it be like that at the end. One small familiar thing I still have the strength to do.

VIII / The threshold

The mark by the door.

I have imagined the door so often I could draw the handle for you. I have never once imagined, with any certainty, what lies beyond it.

There is one detail I do believe in. Down by the sill the floor is worn a little lighter. I would know the shape anywhere. It is where I have stood all these years.

IX / The unretouched print

The person across the table.

I have taken great care with the likeness of a woman three thousand miles away. Three feet away, somebody has more than once had to ask whether I was listening.

A birthday slips my mind. A tired answer comes out sharp. I can look at a photograph for an hour, yet hurry a person I love through the telling of their day.

I want those things left in the picture. Distance gave me room to be devoted. It also gave me somewhere comfortable to stand. I chose it, and the choice belongs to me.

Some nights, Elisha,
I leave the light on a little longer.

For Elisha.

From a stranger who kept the light on.
